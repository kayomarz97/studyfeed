## 5. The offline / no-background guarantee — how it's actually enforced

**Plain:** Three independent locks, so forgetting one still leaves two. The build breaks loudly
rather than shipping an app that can phone home.

**Technical — `verifyOfflineGuarantees`, wired into `check` and as a dependency of every `assemble*`:**

1. **Merged-manifest assertion.** Reads the *merged* manifest (post-`process<Variant>Manifest`, so a
   transitive library that injects a permission or a component cannot hide) and fails on:
   - permissions: `INTERNET`, `ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE`, `ACCESS_WIFI_STATE`, `RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED`,
     `WAKE_LOCK`, `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM`, `USE_EXACT_ALARM`, `FOREGROUND_SERVICE*`
   - components: any `<service>`, any `<receiver>`, any `<provider>` other than none, i.e. an
     allowlist of exactly our `<activity>`. This is what catches WorkManager — it ships its own
     `androidx.startup` provider + `SystemJobService`.
2. **Resolved-classpath denylist.** Walks `runtimeClasspath` resolved artifacts (transitives
   included) and fails on group/module matches: `okhttp`, `retrofit`, `ktor`, `volley`, `apache http`,
   `androidx.work`, `firebase`, `play-services`, `androidx.lifecycle:*-service`, `anthropic`, `openai`.
   Denylist lives in the catalog, one line per entry, with a comment saying why.
3. **Lint baseline-free `lintVitalRelease`** kept green (catches manifest drift AGP already knows about).

**Deliberate design choice:** assertion #2 is a **denylist**, and I'm flagging that honestly — an
allowlist of permitted artifacts would be stricter but would fight every legitimate AndroidX addition
through A1–A7 and get disabled out of frustration, which is worse than a denylist that survives. The
manifest assertion (#1) is the real backstop and *is* an allowlist — nothing can do background work or
open a socket without a permission or component appearing there.

A failing build prints *what* matched and *which* dependency pulled it in (`dependencyInsight`-style
path), not just "denied" — a guard that doesn't say why gets deleted.

## 7. The keystore — §11d, the one that can cost the exam

**Plain:** Android decides "is this an upgrade or a different app?" by the signing key. If we sign
your release APK today, then lose that key file, every future version is a *different app* to your
phone — you'd have to uninstall first, and uninstalling **deletes every mistake, tag, star, streak
and FSRS due-date you've built up**. Weeks before the exam. So: we make it once, we keep it out of
the repo, you back it up somewhere that isn't this laptop, and you tell me you've done it.

**Technical:**
- `keytool` (JBR 21) → `studyfeed-release.jks`, RSA 4096, `-validity 10000`, one key alias.
- Stored **outside the repo** — proposed `C:\Users\kayom\keys\studyfeed\` (gate Q2). Never
  `projects/studyfeed/`, even though `*.jks` is gitignored: belt and braces.
- Passwords → `keystore.properties` (gitignored, next to the keystore, **not** in the repo);
  `app/build.gradle.kts` reads it and **skips the signingConfig gracefully if absent**, so a fresh
  clone can still `assembleDebug`.
- I will print the exact absolute path and tell you plainly to copy it off this machine.
- **⛔ Per §11d, A0 does not close and A1 does not start until you confirm the backup exists.**
- §11e export/import is the second half of this insurance policy — it lands in A1, not here, but it
  is the reason a lost keystore stays survivable.
